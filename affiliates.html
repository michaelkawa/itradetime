What you're actually selling

A $1,000/year membership to a private sourcing network. Members tell us the exact watch they want — reference number, dial, condition — and it gets put out to a network of dealers and traders who quote it at dealer cost, not retail. The pitch in one line: "You don't need a dealer. You need the dealer's group."

One sourced watch typically saves more than the membership costs. That's the math, and it's the only claim you need.

Who buys it

  • Collectors paying retail — they've bought 2–3 watches at AD or grey-market prices and just learned what the trade pays.
  • First serious watch — buying their first AP, Rolex, or Patek and terrified of overpaying or getting a frankenwatch.
  • Flippers — they know a spread when they see one; the group is inventory flow.

How to answer the three objections

"Is this legit?"

Point at the proof page — real quotes, real spreads. Never argue; show.

"Why $1,000?"

One watch. Ask what they last paid, then what the trade pays for the same reference. The gap is usually several times the membership.

"Can't I just DM you when I need one?"

"That's literally what the group is — except the whole network sees your request, not just me." The membership IS the DM, at scale.

The rules that protect all of us

Break any of these and the partnership ends that day.

  • Always disclose. You have a paid relationship with us — say so, every time: "I'm a Watch Cartel partner" or #ad. It's US federal law (FTC), not a style choice.
  • No income or savings guarantees. Never "you'll save $X" or "members make money." Say what the group does; let the proof page carry the numbers.
  • No fake urgency, no fake testimonials, no spam. One honest post beats a hundred bought comments, and platforms detect the difference faster than people do.
  • Don't invent features. No escrow, authentication, or buyback promises beyond what the site itself says. When unsure, link the site instead of answering.

Your money

You earn a share of every membership that comes through your personal link — and it keeps paying as long as they stay members. Your link is issued directly to you; it's how every sale gets credited. No link in your content, no credit — so it goes in every bio, caption and reply you post about us.

The content that converts

  • Receipts beat lifestyle. A screenshot of a real quote vs a retail price outperforms any flex. The 10M-view reel was a price conversation, not a car.
  • Answer questions in public. Every "what's this actually worth?" comment you answer honestly is an ad that doesn't look like one.
  • Your story, one line: what you paid retail for once, and what the group quote was.
